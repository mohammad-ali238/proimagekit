Sharing photos online is great — until someone reposts them without credit. A **watermark** is the simplest way to show who made an image. This guide shows how to add one in under a minute, entirely in your browser.

<Callout type="tip">
  Your photos never leave your device. The watermark is drawn by your own browser, so it works even for private or unreleased images.
</Callout>

## Step 1: Open the watermark tool

Go to the [Add Watermark to Image](/tools/watermark-adder) tool and drop in one or more photos. You can watermark a whole batch at once — every image gets the same settings.

## Step 2: Choose text or a logo

- **Text** — type your name or `© Your Studio`, pick a colour and set the size.
- **Logo** — upload a PNG with a transparent background for the cleanest look.

## Step 3: Position it

Pick one of nine positions, or choose **Tile** to repeat the watermark across the whole picture. Tiled watermarks are much harder to crop out.

| Style | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corner | Portfolios, social posts | Easy to crop away |
| Centre | Previews, proofs | Covers the subject |
| Tiled | Stock photos, client proofs | Most visible |

## Step 4: Fine-tune and download

Adjust **opacity** (40–60% is a good start), rotation and margin. The live preview updates as you go. When it looks right, download each image or everything as a ZIP.

## Tips for a watermark that works

1. **Keep it readable but subtle.** A watermark that dominates the photo distracts from your work.
2. **Use the same watermark everywhere** so people recognise it.
3. **Compress afterwards.** Run the result through the [image compressor](/tools/image-compressor) to make it load faster online.

<Callout type="warning">
  A watermark discourages casual copying, but it can't stop determined misuse. For important work, keep your original, unwatermarked files safe.
</Callout>
